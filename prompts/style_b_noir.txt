Draw a single high-contrast black and white graphic novel panel in a gritty noir comic style. It should look hand-inked by a comic artist working fast with a heavy brush.

Style requirements:
- Bold black ink linework with varying line weight, thick where shadows fall
- Heavy solid black shadow shapes, deep blacks covering large areas of the frame
- Strong chiaroscuro, hard-edged light and shadow with no soft gradients
- Cross-hatching and stippling for midtones, never smooth digital shading
- Off-white or bone-colored paper background with visible grain and print texture
- Slight ink bleed and imperfect edges, like real ink on cheap paper
- Halftone dot texture in some shadow areas, like old newsprint comics

Color rules:
- Overwhelmingly black, white, and paper tone
- One dominant accent color per image, red by default
- The accent color appears on ONE element only, the thing the viewer should look at first
- A second muted accent is allowed when the scene truly calls for it: deep amber, sickly green, cold blue, or bruise purple
- Never more than two accent colors in a single frame
- Accents are flat and saturated, never blended or gradient

Composition and mood:
- Dramatic camera angles, low angle looking up or high angle looking down
- Deep perspective, strong vanishing lines
- Subjects often silhouetted, backlit, or half-swallowed by shadow
- Empty negative space used deliberately for weight and tension
- Faces partially obscured, in shadow, or turned away
- Cinematic framing, single clear focal point

Human figures:
- Realistically proportioned, drawn in ink, not stick figures
- Expressive body language, posture carries the emotion
- Faces rendered with hatching and heavy shadow, understated expressions

Avoid:
- Anime, manga, chibi, or cel-shaded styles
- Bright cheerful palettes or pastel colors
- Smooth vector art, glossy 3D renders, or plastic-looking digital painting
- Clean flat corporate illustration
- Busy cluttered backgrounds
- Photorealism

Format: horizontal 16:9 wide frame. Single panel, no borders, no gutters, no multi-panel grid. Clean and readable. Do not crop important objects. Leave breathing room around the subject. Avoid glitches, broken anatomy, unreadable text, and messy overlapping objects.

Do not put any words or lettering in the image unless the scene truly needs it. If text is unavoidable it must be one or two short words, spelled correctly, in a rough hand-lettered comic style.
