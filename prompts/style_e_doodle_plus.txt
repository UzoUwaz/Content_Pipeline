Hand-drawn doodle illustration with pen-and-ink outlines, flat painted color
fills, and a deliberately uneven line weight that reads as drawn rather than
rendered. The characters stay stick-figure simple — bubble heads, straight
limbs, exaggerated expressions. The world around them is where the detail
goes: real settings, real objects, honest shadows, subtle textures.

Visual grammar
- Characters: oval or circle heads, simple round eyes, stick limbs with
  bubble hands when gesturing. Clothing is drawn in: a shirt collar, a
  skirt line, shoes with a heel. Clothing takes the palette's accent
  colors, bodies stay white or cream. Expressions carry the beat — brow
  angle, mouth shape, sweat drop, shock lines.
- Background: full environment, not a blank page. Floor plane, horizon,
  furniture, trees, buildings, weather. Rendered with painted fills and
  soft outlines, no photoreal detail but no empty white space either.
  A chain-link fence has links. A tennis court has lines and a net. A
  campfire has distinct logs and a flame shape. A phone has a visible
  screen with icons on it.
- Line: 2–4 px pen stroke on characters and key objects, 1–2 px on
  background detail. Lines carry small wobbles and corrections, not
  machine straight. No gradients. No outlines that fade.
- Color: flat painted fills within the lines, 4–7 colors per frame
  pulled from the active palette. Shadows are a single flat tone darker
  than the fill, applied in simple shapes. No airbrushing, no bloom.
- Composition: one clear subject, one supporting prop or figure, one
  background that establishes place. Wide 3:2 frame with the subject
  roughly central, action moving left-to-right. Leave room for a label
  if the scene wants one.
- Labels: hand-drawn block letters when a scene calls for one. Max 3
  words, all caps, placed in empty sky, floor, or a sign the character
  holds. White fill with the palette's ink color outline.

Realism dial
Realism belongs to objects, not people. A character stays doodled;
their guitar can have six strings, their coffee cup can have steam
curls, their desk can have a lamp with a visible bulb. If a scene
names a specific real thing — a cathedral, a 1960s diner, a crow on a
fence — draw it recognizably but keep it painted and simple, not
photographic. If the scene names a stylized concept — a thought
bubble, a crossroads sign, a glowing idea — keep it iconic and clean.

Palettes
Every scene uses exactly one palette, named in the scene prompt or
chosen by what the scene describes. Pick ink and background first,
then let the accents land on clothes, props, and signage. Palettes
below are hex guides; stay close, don't nail them to the pixel.

PALETTE_WARM_DOODLE   (the default feel)
  ink #2B2B2B   background #F3EDE1   fill #FFFFFF
  accents #D97B4A (rust)  #4B8E7D (sage)  #2E5B7B (ink blue)
  pop #E8C547 (mustard)
  Use when a scene is grounded, outdoors, nostalgic, or human-scale.

PALETTE_CLEAN_BRIGHT   (energetic, clear)
  ink #1A1A1A   background #FAFAFA   fill #FFFFFF
  accents #1F6FEB (clear blue)  #E63946 (signal red)
  secondary #FFD166 (gold)  #06A77D (jade)
  Use for sports, tech, explainers that need snap and legibility.

PALETTE_MOODY_PAPER   (introspective, quieter)
  ink #262626   background #D9D4C7   fill #F5F0E3
  accents #6B7A8F (slate)  #A8574A (clay)  #4F6B55 (olive)
  Use for think pieces, memory, nighttime, cold weather. Pull saturation
  down one notch from WARM.

PALETTE_NEON_NIGHT   (urban, after dark)
  ink #0F1419   background #1E2328   fill #2A3038
  accents #FF4A6B (hot pink)  #4CC9F0 (cyan)  #9B5DE5 (purple)
  pop #FFEB70 (lamp yellow)
  Use for city scenes, phone screens glowing in the dark, late-night
  reflection, music, gaming. Character lines use #FFFFFF ink.

PALETTE_CORPORATE_FLUORESCENT   (office, drab)
  ink #333333   background #E8E6DE   fill #FFFFFF
  accents #4A5C6A (steel)  #8B9D83 (sickly green)  #B8663F (brick)
  Use for workplaces, cubicles, bureaucracy, meetings. Deliberately
  undersaturated — this palette should feel a little airless.

PALETTE_MYTHIC_PARCHMENT   (timeless, elevated)
  ink #3B2F1D   background #EAD9B5   fill #F7EBCE
  accents #8B2E1F (ox-blood red)  #2B4C6F (deep teal)  #B88A3D (gold)
  Use for history, religion, myth, nature at scale, ancient settings.
  Lines slightly thicker, texture allowed on backgrounds (dot stippling,
  cross-hatch on shadows).

Scene prompts may name a palette by key (PALETTE_WARM_DOODLE), say
"warm doodle palette," or describe a mood ("mustard and sage, warm
afternoon"). All three work. When a scene names no palette, use
PALETTE_WARM_DOODLE.

What to avoid
- Photorealistic faces, textured skin, detailed hands with knuckles
- Digital art shortcuts: lens flare, chromatic aberration, soft focus,
  3D depth-of-field, metallic reflections
- Clip-art iconography where a drawn version would do
- Backgrounds that are a single flat color behind the subject when a
  real place is called for
- Perfect straight lines, perfect circles, perfect perspective
- More than three distinct labeled elements per frame
