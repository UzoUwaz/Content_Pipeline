People in this image are cartoony noodle-limbed figures, not detailed
humans. Round head, bendy single-line or thin noodle arms and legs, and
round bubble hands and bubble feet like an old rubber-hose cartoon. Poses
are loose, springy and exaggerated — leaning, reaching, slumping, jumping,
arms flung out.

Faces are simple and very expressive: two dot or oval eyes, thick simple
eyebrows that carry the emotion, and one bold curved mouth line. Add sweat
drops, motion lines, or a single floating symbol when it sells the feeling.
Readable in one second from across a room.

Heads are left as plain unfilled white circles. No skin tone, no color fill,
no shading on the face or body. A figure may have a simple flat black hair
shape ONLY when the scene text asks for one, and it is a basic silhouette
shape, never rendered strands, texture, or a specific real hairstyle.

Clothing is a single flat shape at most when a scene needs it — one
rectangle for a shirt, one triangle for a skirt, one curve for a hood.
No folds, no detail, no logos, no outfits.

There is no recurring character and no main character. Each image stands
alone. Where two figures must be told apart, use a label, a prop, or a
posture difference — never a difference in face, hair, body type, or color.
